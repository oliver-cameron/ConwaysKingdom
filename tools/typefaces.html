Kingdom Type Bench

The hotbar and the HUD, rebuilt from the real numbers in views::theme — 44 px slots, 4 px radius, 10 px panel padding, 14 and 12 px text, 13 px key hints — so a typeface can be judged at the size it will actually be read at. Pick a pairing and the whole screen changes.

The pairings

Two faces, because the figures need a monospace: a purse that changes every generation is a number whose width changes with it, and the label under it slides about while the eye re-finds it.

What each buys

Two constraints that are not about taste. The faces are bundled, not asked of the system — a browser has no font to lend, and a client that looked different on every machine makes every screenshot of a bug a screenshot of a different client. And the wasm module is already 7.5 MB shipped, so a face with many weights is real payload. Two weights each is the budget.

IBM Plex Sans / Mono

What is in the tree. Slightly humanist, wide apertures, holds up at 12 px. The mono has the tallest x-height of these, which is why the figures read cleanly small. Nothing is wrong with it.

the baseline

Inter / JetBrains Mono

The most legible small, and the least characterful — Inter is the face every tool ships, so the client would stop looking like itself. JetBrains Mono is a genuine upgrade on the figures: taller digits, clearer 0/O.

worth the mono alone

Space Grotesk / Space Mono

The one with a point of view: narrow, slightly mechanical, odd g and a. Suits a game about machines made of squares. Space Mono is loud in a figure that changes four times a second — pair the Grotesk with a quieter mono.

strongest character

Archivo / Roboto Mono

Grotesque with tight apertures and a condensed sibling, which matters on a hotbar where labels sit under 44 px squares. Roboto Mono is the safe, invisible choice for figures.

best in tight labels

Chivo / Chivo Mono

One superfamily, so the sans and the mono share skeletons and the HUD reads as one voice rather than two. Chivo is a touch heavy at 12 px on a dark ground — check the dim grey labels before committing.

one family, two roles

My pick: Space Grotesk with JetBrains Mono. The client already has a look — dark, square, mechanical — and Plex is the one part of it that could belong to any tool. Grotesk commits; JetBrains keeps the figures boring, which is what a number that changes every generation should be. If that reads as too much, Archivo with JetBrains is the same trade at half the volume.

Overlapping colours

Where the fifteen player colours stand as of today: one fixed table, hue::PALETTE, chosen at once by gradient descent in OKLab for the largest smallest gap, with five of the fifteen allowed to go dark. Each is the swatch colour — a full-saturation texel at lightness 0.62 — and the board places the sheet's shading around it.

Closest pair is 0.169 apart in OKLab, against a floor of 0.16 the test holds. The measurement is straight-line distance in OKLab, the space the table was optimised in, and it is what the_closest_two_players_are_far_enough_apart asserts; the formula this replaced measured 0.045 as the shader drew it. The closest pairs are now among the pale tints — salmon against lilac, sky against mint — where the gamut has the least room. The dark five are the cheapest gap in the table, which is why they are there.
